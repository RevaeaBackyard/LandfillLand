Born from silence.

Snow filled the sky, thin as paper, falling soundlessly into this half-transparent, gray ruin.

At the center of the ruin lay a single human figure.

If the cold wind had not scraped across her skin like an invisible blade, she might not even have been able to confirm that she occupied this space. Snowflakes with perfect geometric forms circled and piled around her, like a group of silent guards protecting their still-unawakened sovereign.

But their master was slow to open her eyes. She still lay quietly, without dream and without sound. She was like a footnote forgotten by this chaotic world, and also like the answer for which the whole world had held its breath and waited for a long time.

The snow kept falling, like a crystalline eternal dream.

In the darkness of that sleep, she seemed to see reflections of the future, crossing many years.

There were hurried crowds, companions walking with her, bright laughter... Lost forest paths wound toward the distance, mountain outlines appeared and vanished in mist, and the noise of teahouses intertwined with the stillness of parks into a picture.

Those beautiful fragments flashed like flowing light, too fast and too urgent for her to grasp.

Until the image suddenly reversed:

She saw the collapsing sky, the falling stars, and the whole world returning to nothingness within some nameless abnormality.

A clear bell rang inside the ruin and broke the long dream.

She slowly opened her eyes. In those sky-blue pupils, the warmth of the dream still remained. She stared blankly at the pure and beautiful world before her.

She did not rise at once.

The cold was not piercing. Instead, it was like a gentle and sluggish veil, covering her consciousness. The dome of the ruin was immeasurably high, and its half-transparent structure refracted gray-white light, as if the building were not made from matter, but condensed from "memory." Snow still fell, but it had no weight. It passed lightly through her hair and turned into tiny points of light on the ground.

The aftersound of the bell still trembled in the air.

It was not a summons and not a warning. It was more like a confirmation: confirming that she had finally awakened, and confirming that the world could continue forward.

She raised her hand, and her fingertips trembled slightly. Wind moved through the cracks of the ruin, carrying scents from an extremely distant place: damp soil, burned-out charcoal, time that had not yet fully cooled. In that instant, she suddenly understood that she had not been "forgotten."

She had been preserved.

For witnessing.

She slowly sat up. Snowflakes drew back around her, as if submitting to an order that had not yet fully revealed itself. Her sky-blue pupils reflected the scene outside the ruin: not a wasteland, but a road that had not yet been laid. The end of the road was hidden by fog, but inside the fog there was light, flickering restlessly.

The fragments that had appeared in her dream emerged once more.

Not as illusions, but as possibilities.

She saw people rebuilding homes above ruins, and people learning again how to sing in a world that had lost its voice. She saw someone walking alone, then being guided at the most exhausted moment by a faint lamp. There were no grand vows and no destined ending, only futures chosen one step at a time.

At last, she stood up.

Behind her, the ruin made tiny cracking sounds, like something disintegrating after completing its mission. The half-transparent walls turned into countless motes of light, rose into the air, and scattered together with the falling snow. For the first time, the world was exposed before her gaze without any reservation.

She took a deep breath.

Then she walked forward.

Not because fate was urging her.

But because she wanted to see with her own eyes where, this time, the world would go.
